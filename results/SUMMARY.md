# AgentGate calibration results

Generated by `bench/plot.py` from `results/calibration.json` (`agentgate calibrate`, 400 resamples per experiment).
Data: the 26 published tau2-bench result files (4 agent models; airline, retail, telecom and telecom variants; 4 trials per task; user simulator gpt-4.1). Nothing here called a model: every number is a resample or reanalysis of trials that already exist.
Gate settings: one-sided alpha 0.05, FAIL when the 90% CI is entirely below -0 points, PASS when it is entirely above -5 points, else INCONCLUSIVE.

## 1. A/A: false alarms when nothing changed

Each file's 4 trials per task split at random into 2 + 2, 400 times. Same agent on both sides, so every FAIL is a false alarm.

- Paired, task-clustered t-test: mean false-FAIL rate **4.7%** across 26 files (range 2.8 to 7.8%; target 5%, binomial SE at 400 resamples is 1.1 points).
- Naive unpaired two-proportion z-test: mean 1.3% (range 0.0 to 7.0%). It is conservative here, not flaky: pairing removes the shared per-task difficulty, so the naive test overstates the variance. The price is power (section 3).
- With only 2 trials per side the gate PASSed an unchanged agent in 54% of splits on average; the rest were INCONCLUSIVE. That is the gate saying 2 trials is not enough to clear a 5-point margin, which section 4 quantifies.

## 4. Trial budget: power analysis and sequential stopping

Parametric model: each retail task keeps its own pass probability from the gpt-4.1 run (Jeffreys posterior mean), B loses 5 or 10 points on every task, 400 simulated gates per budget. The sequential gate looks after every trial with O'Brien-Fleming boundaries for K = budget looks (overall one-sided alpha 0.05).

| budget k | 10 pts: power fixed | power sequential | trials used | 5 pts: power fixed | power sequential | trials used | no change: false FAIL fixed | false FAIL seq | PASS fixed | trials used seq |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 64% | 64% | 1.00 | 30% | 30% | 1.00 | 4.8% | 4.8% | 25% | 1.00 |
| 2 | 86% | 87% | 1.63 | 40% | 40% | 1.91 | 5.8% | 5.2% | 42% | 1.92 |
| 4 | 98% | 98% | 2.44 | 62% | 59% | 3.43 | 4.5% | 5.0% | 65% | 3.35 |
| 8 | 100% | 100% | 3.13 | 89% | 88% | 5.45 | 4.5% | 5.0% | 89% | 5.53 |
| 16 | 100% | 100% | 4.38 | 99% | 97% | 8.12 | 5.8% | 3.5% | 94% | 7.73 |

- A 10-point regression needs about 4 trials per task for 98% power on 114 tasks. Capped at 4, the sequential gate reaches the same power with 2.44 trials per task on average (39% fewer); capped at 8, 3.13 (61% fewer than a fixed 8).
- Savings shrink when the effect is near the threshold (5-point drop) or zero: an unchanged agent at a cap of 4 still uses 3.35 trials, because clearing a 5-point PASS margin needs the evidence.
- Clearing that margin is the expensive part: with 4 trials a no-op change PASSes only 65% of the time (INCONCLUSIVE otherwise); it takes 8 trials to reach 89%.

## 5. Cost and time per gate run (retail, gpt-4.1 agent)

From the trial records: $0.058 agent cost and 63 s per trial (user-simulator cost not included). Baseline results are reused from main, so a gate pays for the candidate side only.

- Fixed 4 trials x 114 tasks: $26.52 and 8.0 trial-hours, about 15 minutes wall-clock with 32 sandboxes in parallel.
- Sequential, capped at 4, on a 10-point regression: $16.14 on average.
- Replay (`replay/`) makes a grader-only change free: no model or tool calls are made when the tool cassette and the trajectories already exist.

Over all default-mode trials, how often each rule fires and the failure rate with and without it. Rules explain failures in the PR comment; the verdict itself uses the task reward (final-state check).

| tool_errors | 85 | 5360 | 67% | 43% |
| write_without_confirmation | 883 | 5360 | 52% | 42% |

`write_without_confirmation` fires often but has the smallest lift: as a PR-comment signal it is weak, and its keyword matcher is the likely reason (users confirm in many ways). Human hand-off and step-budget rules carry real signal.
